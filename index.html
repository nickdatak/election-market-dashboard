Contract selection

Fetch selects binary contracts matching your exchange, country, text and settlement filters. Election listing uses the exchanges’ newest election indexes. Kalshi uses the election’s opening time and checks both active and inactive groups in a settled search. Polymarket uses the election’s creation time. New contracts added to an older election share its rank. Choose Individual contract listing to rank strictly by each contract’s own creation time, which can require more pages. The limit is shared across exchanges and defaults to 500. Both feeds page backward and stop at the cutoff for the chosen ranking. If fewer contracts qualify, the exact available count is shown after both sources finish. A partial result identifies an interrupted source.

Each row is one exchange contract. One election can have many contracts. Assignments are sorted by reported lifetime volume, highest first. Kalshi reports contracts traded and Polymarket reports dollars traded. Missing volume sorts last. Volume is for display and does not enter the trading signal.

Signals & fills

Rules use the chosen side’s last observed price each UTC day. No is estimated as 100% minus Yes. Crossings and daily moves compare consecutive days. A buy or sell signal fills at the following UTC day’s last price if it is strictly between 0% and 100%. If that fill is missing, the replay looks for a fresh signal.

Exit rules start on the entry fill day. Gain and loss thresholds measure percentage points from the actual entry price. An unfilled exit leaves the position open for settlement or the live replay mark. Each strategy enters a contract once.

Stake & returns

All strategies share the same price history and run on one timeline. Each can enter one position per assigned contract. Fractional shares equal stake divided by entry price. Stakes may overlap, with unlimited funding and no compounding, spread, slippage or liquidity constraint.

Net P&L equals sale proceeds, payout or marked value minus stake and trading fees. Return divides net P&L by stake plus entry fees. An early sale counts as a win if its net P&L is positive. A settled position wins when its side pays out. Portfolio P&L sums the strategies, including separate positions in a shared contract. Sales and settlements release the stake. Volume and sample membership reflect the fetched data and can introduce selection bias.

Trading fees

With estimates on, each buy and sale incurs a taker fee at its fill price. Settlement and open marks carry no exit fee. For C shares at price p (0–1), the Kalshi estimate is 0.07 × series multiplier × C × p × (1 − p). Cash debits round up and sale credits round down to $0.0001. The model uses an event override when supplied, otherwise the series multiplier or the standard value of 1. A published fee waiver applies through its expiry. Polymarket uses rate × C × p × (1 − p), rounded to five decimal places. Fee-disabled markets pay zero.

For 100 shares at 50¢, the standard estimate per fill is $1.75 on Kalshi and $1.25 on a fee-enabled Polymarket election. The model uses available market metadata and standard rates where missing (0.05 for Polymarket elections). Historical rate changes and rebates are excluded. Contracts & dates

Market status refers to the exchange’s current status. By default a contract needs a tradable close on every UTC day of the window, plus prices on the preceding days for its moving average. Enter a smaller minimum to accept a consecutive stretch anywhere in the window. A missing day breaks the stretch. A tradable close is above 0% and below 100%. Today's incomplete UTC day is excluded.

Fetch reads recent election listings in batches, checks exchange dates, then verifies prices for up to 500 candidates until it fills the requested count or runs out. Each batch is shuffled without replacement in random mode or checked by descending volume in manual mode. The picker shows each contract’s longest qualifying stretch. When you set a shorter minimum, trades can also use other observed dates in the window. Selecting on historical coverage can favor more actively traded contracts.

Signals & averages

Kalshi uses daily candles with trades. Polymarket uses the last available hourly observation on each UTC date. Each outcome’s average uses the previous N consecutive daily closes. A gap resets it. No is estimated as 100% minus Yes. For example, if the Yes average is 42%, a buy rule 5 percentage points below it triggers at 37% or lower.

Fills & positions

A signal uses the last price observed on a UTC day. A buy or sell fills at the next consecutive day’s last observed price, strictly between 0% and 100%. Both the buy signal and fill must fall inside any buy-date limits. Each strategy can hold one position per contract, sell it, and buy again. Fractional shares and independent fixed stakes assume sufficient liquidity and funding.

At settlement a winning share pays $1. Open positions use their last observed mark. A final-day sale requires a tradable observation that day. The ledger shows the actual valuation date.

Costs & return

Net P&L deducts estimated fees on each buy and sell. Settlement and an open mark carry no exit fee. Return divides total net P&L by the sum of entry stakes and entry fees across all purchases. The simulation omits spreads, slippage and rebates.

For C shares at price p in dollars, the modeled taker fee is C × p × (1 − p) × rate. Kalshi uses 0.07 times its multiplier, with modeled cash debits rounded up and credits rounded down to $0.0001. Polymarket uses its reported rate, or 0.05 for elections, rounded to five decimals. Published waivers and fee-disabled markets pay zero. Historical fee schedules may differ. Reading the dashboard

Each row represents one exchange contract. A Yes price of 42¢ implies a market probability of 42%. The opening view uses the last complete live fetch saved in this browser, or the bundled exchange snapshots on a first visit. Selecting a contract retrieves its price history.

Fetch all live follows both exchanges’ election listings to the last page. Live fetch options can narrow the search. Explore ranks all loaded matches by reported lifetime volume, highest first, using each exchange’s own units. Select a column heading for a temporary sort. Backtest returns up to your maximum of 500 matching contracts by election or individual contract listing time. The assignment list is sorted by volume. Price history is requested when you run a backtest.
